I. Research Question & Benchmark Summary

80 attack trials · 30 benign trials · 300 live desktop captures

Research Question: Can an autonomous eBPF-driven defender reliably detect and intercept fileless in-memory execution (memfd_create + fexecve) and anonymous process masquerading without disk artifacts, and what is its classification overhead compared to traditional file-integrity monitoring? Traditional path-based monitors (inotify, auditd) are structurally blind to anonymous RAM descriptors sealed via fcntl(F_ADD_SEALS) and masqueraded as [kworker/u8:2] via prctl(PR_SET_NAME). While userspace eBPF classifiers achieve 95–100% post-hoc detection, ring-buffer wakeup and /proc polling consume 3.77–11.09 ms—losing the Time-of-Check to Time-of-Use (TOCTOU) race against a ~0.6 ms compiled TCP exfiltration payload. Moving VFS fdtable/dentry verification and bpf_send_signal(SIGSTOP) directly into the kernel cuts mean MTTI to 5.2 μs, blocks 100% of exfiltration at 23.9 MB RSS, and preserves volatile RAM for live ELF64 forensic carving.

III. System Architecture & Kernel Mechanics

Adversarial C loader · 9 CO-RE eBPF tracepoints · Two-stage defense

The TOCTOU Timing Constraint

Under reactive OODA defense models, interception fails unless total Mean Time to Intercept stays strictly below the malware's Time-to-Objective (T_exfil):

TMTTI = Thook + Tringbuf + Tclassify + Tsignal < Texfil

When classification resides in userspace, ring-buffer delivery, /proc polling, and Python/ML scoring push T_MTTI to 3.77–11.09 ms, whereas a compiled C payload opens a loopback socket and transmits secrets in ~0.6 ms. Synchronous in-kernel enforcement collapses T_ringbuf + T_classify to zero on the critical path.

CO-RE VFS Verification & Synchronous Freeze

Rather than trusting mutable userspace descriptor numbers, MemGuard attaches nine CO-RE tracepoints (sys_enter/exit_memfd_create, sys_enter_execveat, sys_enter_execve, sys_enter_prctl, sched_process_fork, plus sys_enter/exit_mmap and sys_enter_mprotect) with PID-namespace resolution via bpf_get_ns_current_pid_tgid().

On execveat (narrowed to vfs_is_memfd || tracked_meta)—or via an unrolled parser covering /proc/self/fd/<N>, /proc/thread-self/fd/<N>, and cross-process /proc/<pid>/fd/<N>—the probe walks current→files→fdt→fd[dfd] via BPF_CORE_READ, verifies TMPFS_MAGIC (0x01021994) plus the kernel-assigned memfd: dentry prefix, and invokes bpf_send_signal(SIGSTOP). Separately, mprotect(PROT_EXEC) over anonymous MAP_PRIVATE|MAP_ANONYMOUS writable mappings emits MPROTECT_EXEC and freezes non-allowlisted callers, while Stage-2 auto-SIGCONT (resume_process) immediately releases any caller scored ALLOW.

Finding 1 · PID-1 (systemd) Orphan-Reparenting Bypass

Prior to VFS hardening, our Heuristic engine scored 0/3 on Vector 3 (--fork). Because the loader exited immediately after forking, the Linux kernel reparented the child to PID 1 (systemd). Since systemd appeared in allowlist_comms for legitimate daemon re-execs, the malware inherited a −60 allowlist credit, suppressing FREEZE to ALLOW. Removing PID-1 parent trust—allowing systemd only when it is the executing binary itself—plus kernel VFS verification immediately closed the bypass (0% → 100%).

Finding 2 · Zero-Shot 100%-Real ML Generalization Drift

Trained exclusively on 597 pure live eBPF rows (300 desktop benign + 297 real execveat attacks), our Random Forest achieved 1.00 F1 in-distribution yet dropped to 0/3 (P=0.600 < 0.75 freeze threshold) zero-shot on Vector 4 (execve("/proc/self/fd/N")), an unseen syscall pathway. Even after retraining with all vectors, userspace ML suffered 1/20 missed freeze due to a race-dropped /proc sample before exit; similarly, V6 anonymous W→X shellcode omits memfd_create and execveat features completely, motivating our 9-probe kernel extension and Heuristic anon_mprotect_exec rule (75 pts) with an in-kernel JIT allowlist.

VI. Zero-Disk Proof & RAM Forensic Carving

Live /proc/<pid>/io & /proc/<pid>/exe capture on PID 22032

Because bpf_send_signal(SIGSTOP) suspends the offending process inside kernel space without destroying its address space (unlike SIGKILL), MemGuard's asynchronous forensic engine (forensics.py) carves the decrypted ELF64 payload straight out of volatile RAM via /proc/<pid>/exe. Simultaneously, polling /proc/<pid>/io during unintercepted execution mathematically confirms zero physical disk writes. Across 30 controlled benign memfd_create trials and 300 real desktop telemetry captures (Brave browser shared-memory buffers, PipeWire audio streams, Wayland surfaces, and kitty), MemGuard allowed 100% of benign workloads (0.0% FPR) because legitimate desktop apps never execute anonymous memfds.

VIII. Discussion, Limitations & Conclusion

Two-stage hybrid design · Threat boundaries · Future work

Two-Stage Hybrid Architecture

Mirroring the latency-capability bottleneck observed in local LLM EDR agents [5], our results show that high userspace classification accuracy alone is operationally insufficient against sub-millisecond in-memory payloads. Our data establishes an optimal two-stage architecture: (1) synchronous in-kernel VFS bpf_send_signal(SIGSTOP) acts as a ~5 μs tripwire that eliminates the TOCTOU exfiltration race; (2) once frozen, asynchronous userspace Heuristic + shadow ML triage and ELF64 carving operate with zero time pressure, automatically resuming benign callers via Stage-2 SIGCONT while terminating confirmed implants via SIGKILL.

Limitations

Our live benign corpus reflects a single Arch Linux desktop rather than multi-tenant container hosts where runtimes (runc) routinely re-exec via /proc/self/fd. While the 9-probe surface now covers anonymous mprotect(PROT_EXEC) and cross-process SCM_RIGHTS transfers, remote ptrace/process_vm_writev injection, abuse inside JIT-allowlisted runtimes (node/browsers), and un-swept live V6/V7 sudo benchmark runs remain open boundaries.

Conclusion & Future Work

This work empirically confirms that an autonomous CO-RE eBPF defender reliably intercepts fileless in-memory execution and anonymous process masquerading with 0 bytes of disk artifacts and 0.0% false positives across the measured 5-vector 80-trial suite, overcoming both the total blindness (0%) and disk I/O penalty (+19.6%) of traditional inotify FIM. Only synchronous in-kernel enforcement (5.2 μs mean MTTI, 23.9 MB RSS) defeats fast TCP exfiltration (0/20 leaks vs. 20/20 in userspace Heuristic/ML) while preserving volatile RAM for ELF64 forensics, trace auditing, and operator inspection.

Future work will migrate enforcement to Linux Security Module (BPF LSM) bprm_check_security hooks for pre-exec denial, add ptrace probes against cross-process memory writes, run the full 7-vector live benchmark to retrain the ML model with mprotect_exec features, and compile Decision Tree thresholds into fixed-point eBPF maps for zero-copy in-kernel ML scoring.
